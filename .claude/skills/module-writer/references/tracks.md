# Tracks: syllabi and per-track guidance

The syllabus (module numbers, titles, one-line summaries, capstone) for all four tracks lives in `content/tracks.json`; that file also drives the app's track pages. Add or reorder modules there first, then write the markdown. Each track has 10-15 modules plus a capstone; currently 13 + capstone each (14 + capstone for film).

IDs: `<prefix>-NN`, with prefixes `re`, `sb`, `hc`, `mbk`. File names: `content/<track-id>/NN-slug.md`.

## real-estate (Real Estate)

Voice: a long-time owner-operator of multifamily and small commercial. Numbers: price per door/foot, NOI, cap rates, debt terms, renovation budgets, turn costs, tax mechanics.
Rules: give market-dependent figures as ranges with an era; use the same recurring cast of illustrative deals across modules so the learner can compare; call out that tax law and lending terms change.
Typical hidden problems to surface in examples: seller pro forma vs. T-12, expense under-reporting, property-tax reassessment on sale, deferred maintenance, concentration in one tenant, floating-rate exposure, insurance repricing.

## small-business (Small Business Acquisition)

Voice: a veteran deal-maker and operator who has bought, run, and sold main-street businesses ($500k-$5M of earnings). Numbers: SDE vs. EBITDA, multiples by size and quality, working capital, SBA 7(a) structure, DSCR, equity injection, seller notes.
Rules: SBA program rules (loan limits, equity injection minimums, seller-note standby requirements, eligibility) change; teach the mechanism with the current structure as of the era written and tell the learner to confirm current SOP terms with the lender before relying on them. Don't invent named brokers or marketplaces' stats.
Typical hidden problems: add-backs that are really recurring costs, owner who is the business, customer concentration, deferred capex, cash-vs-accrual revenue gaps, lease assignment problems, licenses that don't transfer.

## holdco (HoldCo Structure & Credit)

Voice: a veteran who has run a family holding company and sat on both sides of the credit table (borrower and lender). Numbers: leverage multiples, coverage ratios, covenant headroom, waterfall math, intercompany balances, consolidated vs. standalone cash flow.
Rules: legal and tax structure varies by state and facts; teach the logic (why a structure exists, what it protects against, how courts and lenders treat it) and name the specialist to involve. Emphasize that liability separation is only as good as the discipline (separate books, arm's-length intercompany agreements, no commingling).
Typical hidden problems: veil-piercing from commingling, cross-defaults, upstream guarantees, dividend blockers, covenants tripped by one OpCo, double taxation, key-person dependence.

## mbk-film (MBK Productions: Filmmaking)

Voice: a working filmmaker/teacher with decades across writing, directing, acting and producing, who has made things with almost no money and has seen what makes sets collapse. Warm, craft-obsessed, blunt about finishing.
"Real numbers" here means: page counts and running time (about a page a minute), pages shot per day (no-budget realistic range roughly 2-5 pages per day), shooting ratio, number of company moves, day-rate and kit-rental ranges, release-form and permit realities, scene beat breakdowns, and before/after rewrites of actual lines of dialogue. Worked examples can be schedules, a budget top sheet, a shot list for a specific scene, a scene rewrite, or a direction-to-actor exchange.
Rules: writing and acting exercises should be doable alone or with one friend. When giving craft opinions, label taste vs. craft ("this is taste; the craft part is..."). Don't quote or reproduce real screenplays beyond a line or two; invent scenes instead. Music/footage rights and permits are legal topics: teach the principle and say when to get a real answer from a lawyer or the licensor.
Typical hidden problems: scripts written for locations/budgets you can't get, overambitious page counts per day, bad sound, no coverage plan, unreleased locations and actors, losing the crew to an exhausting schedule, no finishing plan.
Quiz style: scenario questions fit well here ("You have 6 pages, 4 hours of light, and one actor leaves at 2pm. What do you shoot first?").

## Capstones (all tracks)

A realistic case worked end to end in three stages with a mid-course surprise that changes the answer. The capstone must reuse concepts from at least eight earlier modules and the quiz should force trade-offs (not recall). Provide full numbers for the base case in Stage 1, then change a key input in Stage 2 or 3 and make the learner re-decide.
